# Literature check: is there a third option better than both a graph network and tuned trees for shear modulus?

Run on 2026-11-27 for the shear-modulus recipe decision (`docs/DECISIONS.md` DEC-021). Three search agents and one verifying judge used
the free connectors only (FastTrack Literature Open, alphaXiv, SciSpace; Consensus was tried five times, four times by the search agents (see Searches run) and once by the judge, and
every call answered that its free usage for the billing period was used up, so it returned no papers; paid usage was not
turned on). The judge looked up every paper (Crossref through FastTrack, or the full text
on alphaXiv) and re-checked the quoted numbers where the text was available. This file is generated from their structured output;
summaries are the agents', not the papers' own words. A paper not found is not evidence that none exists.

**Judge's answer:** no supporting evidence.

**Strongest published candidate:** The strongest published candidate is not a blend of our two models. It is a descriptor model fed latent features from pretrained GNNs and interatomic potentials (MatterVial: MODNet on Matminer + MEGNet/ORB/ROOST/SISSO features; HackNIP: ORB embeddings + MODNet). MatterVial reports log10 G_VRH MAE 0.032 against 0.067 (coGN) and 0.073 (MODNet) on random 5-fold Matbench folds of about 11k MP crystals. It has not been tested under a held-out-chemical-system split, it depends on pretrained models whose training data (including MEGNet trained on MP G_VRH) probably overlap the test labels, and its downstream learner is a neural net, not trees.

**Reasoning:**

All 25 distinct papers exist (Crossref or full-text checks), and the numbers I re-checked match what the earlier agents reported (MatterVial Table 1 and 2, Gong Table S1, MODNet Table 1, ALIGNN Table 3, Omee Table 4, Stepurko Tables S4/S6/S22, Parker Table 1).

What the papers say, taken together:
(1) No returned paper tests a post-hoc average or stack of a CGCNN-type network with tuned descriptor trees for elastic moduli on any split. My own searches (alphaXiv discover, FastTrack search, SciSpace) found none either.
(2) Every paper where a third option beats both a GNN and a descriptor model on shear modulus used random Matbench/MP splits:
- MatterVial: 0.032 vs 0.067 coGN and 0.073 MODNet.
- MODNet: 0.0731 vs 0.0849 Automatminer trees and 0.0895 CGCNN.
- Gong et al.: de-CGCNN 11.1 vs CGCNN 12.2 vs RF 15.0 GPa.
Each has a limitation that blocks transfer to our setting:
- MatterVial's gain comes from pretrained features. Its authors say large Matbench tasks are no longer independent test sets for pretrained models, and its MEGNet featurizers were trained on MP G_VRH. When it fused a GNN trained only on the task (coGN, perovskites), it did not beat that GNN (0.0313 vs 0.0269).
- In Gong et al., the tree baseline is a weak 29-descriptor RF that loses to both GNNs, so 'beats both' just means it beats the GNN. The gain was 3-9% on G, about zero on K, on one split with no confidence intervals.
- MODNet is a different learner (a descriptor NN), not a combination, and was compared against AutoML trees, not our features.
(3) The papers closest to our split type show trees holding up or winning under chemistry holdouts and random-split GNN leaders degrading badly OOD (Li 2023/2025, Omee 2024). None tests a combination. IM-Fuse reports only 'stability', for a different property, from the abstract.
(4) On JARVIS (our database), descriptor trees roughly tie CGCNN on Gv: 11.98 vs 11.75 GPa (ALIGNN paper). Matbench Automatminer trees beat CGCNN on log_gvrh: 0.0849 vs 0.0895. Both agree with our trees > CGCNN.
(5) Other evidence for blends is off-domain: molecules (Parker; liquid crystals) or band gap. There the combination wins on some targets and loses on others.

How much weight each piece deserves: our measured result carries the most (blend vs trees -0.00158 [-0.00432, +0.00113], no clear difference). MatterVial gets low to moderate weight: same property, but the split is random, the pretraining overlaps, and it is a different kind of option. MODNet, Gong, HackNIP, Kim 2026 and IM-Fuse get low weight. Li OOD and Omee get moderate weight as context that weakens transfer of random-split gains. Parker, Rahman and the band-gap and liquid-crystal papers get very low weight.

Conclusion: no returned paper, in a setting reasonably close to ours (shear or elastic modulus, about 10^4 crystals, chemistry-held-out split, tuned rich-feature trees as the baseline), shows a third option more accurate than both a graph network and tuned descriptor trees. The random-split hybrid results are real but do not address our split or our blend.
