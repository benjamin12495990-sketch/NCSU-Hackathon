What I did not find
Method and added-salt columns: none of the three states the salt concentration on its landing page. You would have to read the files.

Γmax, area per molecule, Krafft point, pC20, cloud point: the only open records are SURFPRO, which you already have, and the PendantProp zip. SURFPRO is described as including Tmax, pC20 and other properties. For cloud point and Krafft point I found only papers, such as Schott on polyoxyethylated nonionic surfactants, and no data record.

Detergency with composition: I found no open record. The hits were patents with soil-removal tables (for example US4259217A ) and paper tables, such as the dishwashing study in Molecules. Patents and paper tables would have to be text-mined. The EMPA/EN 60456 laundry study I found gives only ΔL* results, not a downloadable dataset.

Papers without a data record
SurfPro2 (ChemRxiv 10.26434/chemrxiv.15006392): describes a relational database of experimental surfactant properties. I could not verify a data link, so check whether it extends SURFPRO.

Multi-task GNN for surfactant properties (Digital Discovery, 2026): covers pC20 and related properties. I did not confirm a data link.

Scholz, Behnke and Resch-Genger, J. Fluoresc. 2018: compares fluorometry, conductometry and surface tension for CMC. I did not check for a data record.

Hödl et al. curated database paper (ScienceDirect, 2025): this is the SURFPRO paper. You already have SURFPRO.

What I'd add first
PendantProp (Zenodo 17232763). It is the only record with a CC BY 4.0 licence and raw isotherms from a single lab, so Γmax and γCMC come from one protocol. Check the zip for temperature and salt columns before relying on it.

P2SURF. It is on GitHub with SMILES and experimental CMC values. Read the LICENSE file first, and deduplicate against Qin and SURFPRO.

Kleinheins et al. (Zenodo 10550607). It is small, but it is the only record here that may include salt as a variable. Read the README first to see whether it holds single-surfactant data at all.

Data-quality traps
Unit mix: P2SURF reports log CMC in µM. You want pCMC in mol/L, so convert.

Duplicate rows: P2SURF builds on Qin et al., and Adygamov 2026 also uses Qin. Deduplicate on canonical SMILES plus temperature before merging.

Mixed methods: Adygamov 2026 filtered NIST rows by method, keeping tensiometry and conductivity, to avoid mixing techniques. Do the same, and record the method per row.

Salt and water type: tap versus deionised water shifted measured CMCs, and conductivity failed in tap water. Treat added salt as a required input.

Computed versus measured: I did not verify the P2SURF and Kleinheins records, so check each file for predicted values.
