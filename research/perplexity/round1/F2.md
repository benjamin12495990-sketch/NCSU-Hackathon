What I'd add first
Zenodo 23215479. It is the only candidate with explicit tempering time and temperature, but verify the licence and scale before use.

Raiipa tempering data. It is openly licensed, has composition plus time and temperature, and its conversions are documented.

The PMD Vickers records. They follow ISO 6507, but they are copper alloys and I haven't opened them, so check whether they fit your scope.

Data-quality traps
Mixed scales and conversions in Raiipa: the final-hardness column is HRC. The page says 82 low-hardness rows (below 238 HV) were converted using the ASTM E140-12b formula. Per the page, that table is valid only from 238 to 940 HV, so these rows sit outside it.

Inconsistent measurement counts: the source papers averaged different numbers of measurements per sample.

Missing values: 949 rows, all from Grange, have no initial hardness.

Composition fixes: trace Ni and Mo were set to 0, and a SAE 6140/6145 label conflict was resolved by approximation.

Possible overlap: this dataset and the Kang 2014 data share two sources (Hollomon and Grange).

Test-load effects: hardness falls as load rises, for example 622.6 HV at 100 gf against 587.1 HV at 2000 gf in one RHEA. Include load as a feature or restrict the load range.

Predicted values: HEA papers often mix measured and model-predicted hardness in the same tables, so keep a "source of value" flag.
