For ASTM E1356, divide the 95% limits by 2.8 to get standard deviations. The standard also says no bias can be determined because there are no reference values.

The ASTM C39 figures come from two unofficial copies that disagree on some digits. One lists 2.9% and 3.2% as the laboratory CVs, and the other lists 2.4%. I used the table from the 2005 text.

For E1876, modulus scales with the square of frequency. So the modulus CV would be roughly double the frequency CV. That is my own inference, not a published value.

Rules of thumb and qualitative results
Corrosion by immersion (ASTM G31): The practice says the spread between duplicate specimens "probably should not exceed ±10%" of the average for uniform attack. This is a guideline, not a measured study. I found no interlaboratory reproducibility figure.

Ionic conductivity of solid electrolytes: Round-robin studies exist for thiophosphate argyrodites (Ohno et al., ACS Energy Lett. 2020) and for oxide LATP/LLZO pellets (J. Power Sources). I retrieved no numeric standard deviation or lab count for either.
