Add first
Blended Cement Concrete Database. It is the only record I found with your full input list plus curing temperature and humidity (your priority 2) and a source DOI per row. That DOI lets you de-duplicate against UCI and your other sets. The CC BY-NC licence blocks commercial use.

Pavement Concrete Datasets. It comes from transport-agency records (priority 3) and is CC BY 4.0. It has no curing columns, and its age range is unchecked.

UHPC mix-design database. It is the only one with per-row specimen geometry (priority 4) and curing regime. UHPC is a different regime from the 20–80 MPa UCI data, so keep it as a separate domain feature or a separate model.

Data-quality traps
Mixed specimens: The literature compilations mix cubes and cylinders. The UHPC database says it never converts between them, so a strength column there is not one consistent quantity.

Duplicates: The literature-derived sets can contain mixes already in UCI, in your Mendeley sets, or in each other. Use the source DOI columns to de-duplicate before you split train and test.

Incomplete columns: Missing values are null or blank, for example silica fume and superplasticizer. Don't fill them with 0.

Extraction errors: The UHPC database blanked 111 elastic moduli that were steel-fibre values mis-mapped into the concrete field, and 40 values moved from slump to flow-spread. Expect similar errors in the other extracted sets.

Non-destructive set: The Matthews NDT set is already on your list. I saw an older 2025 version (10.5281/zenodo.14921019) with 16,531 test results and strengths normalised to a 150×300 mm cylinder. The NDT results are inputs, not mix proportions.

Computed values: I saw none. All the records I opened are experimental or extracted from experiments.
